## Assignment 2: Good and Bad Data Visualization

### Requirements:

- Data visualizations are important tools for communication and convincing; we need to be able to evaluate the ways that data are presented in visual form to be critical consumers of information 
- To test your evaluation skills, locate two public data visualizations online, one good and one bad  
    - You can find data visualizations at https://public.tableau.com/app/discover or https://datavizproject.com/, or anywhere else you like! 
- For each visualization (good and bad):  
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
      GOOD VISUALIZATION: Grouped Bar Chart (https://datavizproject.com/data-type/grouped-bar-chart/) 
      Aesthetic: Is the visualization pleasing to look at?
      This visualization is visually appealing because it is simple and organized. The bars are evenly spaced, and the colors help distinguish the groups without being distracting. There are no unnecessary effects or decorations, which makes the chart easy to read. 
      
      Substantive: Does the visualization accurately and honestly present data?
      This visualization presents the data clearly and accurately. The lengths of the bars represent the values, and all bars start from the same baseline, making comparisons fair and easy. A grouped bar chart is an appropriate choice when the goal is to compare different groups across categories.  

      Perceptual: Can we understand what message the maker is trying to convey?
      The general message is easy to recognize - i.e. that the chart is intended to compare multiple groups across categories. The side-by-side bars make comparisons straightforward. However, the lack of a legend and descriptive labels makes it difficult to determine exactly what the groups represent, limiting our ability to fully understand the intended message. 

         

      BAD VISUALIZATION: Chord Diagram (https://datavizproject.com/data-type/chord-diagram/)
      
      Aesthetic: Is the visualization pleasing to look at?
      The visualization is visually attractive because of its circular layout and colorful connecting ribbons. The design immediately catches our attention and appears sophisticated. However, the large number of overlapping lines creates visual clutter, making the chart look busy and difficult to follow.
      
      Substantive: Does the visualization accurately and honestly present data?
      The visualization seems to show the relationships between different categories correctly, but it is difficult to compare the connections because the curved ribbons are harder to interpret than simple bars or lines. This makes it challenging to see which relationships are stronger or more important. In addition, there is no title, legend, or explanation of the colors, so it is not clear what the chart is showing. Without this information, the visualization is difficult to fully understand and use.
      
      Perceptual: Can we understand what message the maker is trying to convey?
      This is the weakest aspect of the visualization. Although we can tell that connections exist between categories, it is difficult to identify which relationships are strongest or most important. The overlapping ribbons require considerable effort to trace, and we may struggle to compare values across categories. As a result, the intended message is not immediately clear. Also, there is no title explaining the purpose of the chart, and there is no legend to identify the meaning of the colors or connections. 
            
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      For GOOD VISUALIZATION example above: Grouped Bar Chart (https://datavizproject.com/data-type/grouped-bar-chart/) 
      The grouped bar chart is an appropriate visualization for comparing multiple groups across categories. However, adding a title, legend, descriptive axis labels, and more meaningful category names would greatly improve its interpretability. These changes would help us understand not only the differences between bars, but also what those differences represent.
      
      For BAD VISUALIZATION: Chord Diagram (https://datavizproject.com/data-type/chord-diagram/)
      The chord diagram could be improved by adding a clear title and a legend so we know what the data shows. It also needs clearer labels for the categories. The chart would be easier to read if it showed fewer connections or only the most important ones. If it is interactive, features like hovering or filtering would also help. Overall, these changes would make the chart less cluttered and easier to understand.
      ```
